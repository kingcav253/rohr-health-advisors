Nevada has a public option, and it's working

Nevada launched Battle Born State Plans for 2026 — a state public option, federally approved, sold through Nevada Health Link alongside private carriers. They cover the same essential health benefits every ACA plan does, and they're required to hit specific premium reduction targets.

Nevadans noticed immediately. About one in five people who actively shopped chose a public option plan, and they accounted for roughly 10% of total marketplace enrollment in their first year.

The more interesting effect is on everyone else. State analysis indicates rates across the whole Nevada market came in about 4.6% lower than they would have been without the public option applying competitive pressure. If you buy any plan through Nevada Health Link, you're paying less because Battle Born exists.

Even so, Nevada absorbed a real increase: roughly 22% overall for 2026, and about 26% for plans that already existed. Marketplace enrollment fell nearly 6%.

Short-term coverage in Nevada is tightly limited

Nevada caps short-term plans at 185 days, and once a policy expires you cannot re-apply during that same calendar year. It's a genuine one-time bridge, not a rolling alternative — and considerably stricter than states where total duration can reach three years.

If you're between jobs in Nevada, that constraint shapes your options, and it's worth knowing before you plan around it.

Where the subsidy line falls

Premium tax credits end above $63,840 for one person and $132,000 for a household of four in 2027. Below that line, a subsidised Marketplace plan — possibly a Battle Born plan — is usually the strongest value in Nevada, and I'll tell you so.

Above the line you're paying full price, and comparing the entire market becomes worth the time. Nevada also expanded Medicaid, so there's no coverage gap here for adults at lower incomes.
