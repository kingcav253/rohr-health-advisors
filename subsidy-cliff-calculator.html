How the cliff works

Premium tax credits phase in gradually as income falls. They do not phase out gradually as income rises. Above 400% of the federal poverty level, they stop — completely, at a single dollar.

That structure was suspended for several years. The American Rescue Plan removed the cap in 2021, the Inflation Reduction Act extended that through 2025, and on January 1, 2026 the extension expired. The original cliff came back.

Why this matters more if you're self-employed

Two reasons, and they compound. A W-2 employee knows what they'll earn. A freelancer or 1099 contractor is estimating next year's income before the year happens — and the estimate determines the subsidy. Second, self-employed people who've built something real often earn between $60,000 and $130,000, which is exactly where the cliff sits for most household sizes.

The useful part: the cliff is measured on modified adjusted gross income, not revenue. For someone a few thousand dollars over the line, a retirement contribution can be worth far more than its own tax saving, because it can restore a premium tax credit worth thousands. That's a rare case where one decision pays twice.

I'm a licensed broker, not a CPA, so I won't tell you which deductions to take. What I can do is tell you precisely where your line is and what your premium looks like on either side of it. That's a much more productive thing to walk into your accountant's office holding.

What this calculator deliberately doesn't do

It doesn't estimate your premium. Plenty of tools will, and most of them are wrong, because an accurate number requires your ZIP code, your age, everyone's tobacco status, and the actual plans filed in your county this year.

So this does one thing exactly rather than several things approximately. Your threshold is a fixed function of household size — that I can compute to the dollar. Your premium isn't, and I'd rather tell you that than hand you a confident-looking number that falls apart at enrollment.

Questions people ask

What counts toward MAGI?

Adjusted gross income plus any tax-exempt interest, non-taxable Social Security benefits, and excluded foreign income. For most self-employed households it's very close to the AGI line on your return.

What if my income is unpredictable?

Estimate honestly and revisit it during the year. You can update your projected income with the Marketplace as things change, which adjusts your credit going forward instead of leaving a surprise at tax time.

What if I go over the line by accident?

Credits are reconciled on your return, and crossing 400% FPL can mean repaying what you received as a lump sum. This is why guessing low is a bad strategy — it doesn't create a subsidy, it defers a bill.

Does being above the cliff mean I have no options?

No. It means the Marketplace has lost its structural price advantage for you, so comparing the whole market becomes worth doing. What's actually available depends on your state and your health history.
