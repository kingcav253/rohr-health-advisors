ACA / Marketplace Plans

Federal and state health insurance plans available through the Affordable Care Act. Subsidies based on income can dramatically reduce your monthly premium — sometimes to $0.

  • Income-based tax credits available
  • Pre-existing conditions always covered
  • Preventive care at no extra cost
  • Open enrollment + qualifying life events
  • Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum tiers
Best for: Most self-employed individuals and those with moderate income

Private Health Plans

Plans purchased directly from insurance carriers, outside the Marketplace. Often offer lower premiums or access to broader provider networks — depending on your health profile.

  • Potentially lower monthly premiums
  • More flexibility in plan design
  • Wider carrier and network options
  • Underwriting may apply
  • No subsidy eligibility
Best for: Healthy individuals who earn too much for subsidies
Get a comparison
⏱️

Short-Term / Bridge Coverage

Temporary coverage for people between jobs, waiting for open enrollment, or in a coverage gap. Not a long-term solution, but better than nothing when you need a bridge.

  • Fast approval, often within 24 hours
  • Lower premiums than full coverage
  • Flexible term lengths
  • Limited benefits vs. ACA plans
  • Pre-existing conditions may not be covered
Best for: Gaps in coverage, transitions between jobs

The honest truth

The right plan depends entirely on your situation.

Income, health history, where you live, how many people you're covering, and what you can spend monthly — all of it matters. That's why a 20-minute conversation is worth more than any online comparison tool.

What if I make too much for subsidies?

A private plan might genuinely cost less. Cavin will run both numbers before recommending anything.

What if I have a pre-existing condition?

ACA plans cover pre-existing conditions — always. A private plan might not. That changes the math significantly.

What about my doctor — will they be in-network?

Cavin checks before recommending a plan. Keeping your doctor matters, and it's part of the comparison process.

Is there really no cost to me for using a broker?

Correct. Brokers are compensated by the insurance carrier, not by you. The price of a plan is the same whether you use a broker or enroll directly.
